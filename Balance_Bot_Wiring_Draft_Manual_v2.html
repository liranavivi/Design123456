Balance Bot Build Manual v2

Step-by-step instructions for the four-board stack: 0 motors · 1 battery · 2 power (both MP1584 + TB6612) · 3 logic (Pro Mini, MPU6050, HC-SR04 and HC-05 sockets). Every cable rises at the back. Based on the v2 layout page and HANDOVER.md section 9.3l. Tick each step as you go; the ticks are saved in this browser only.

Draft for approval: wiring drawings and color code, 4 Nov 2026

Robot color code: one meaning per color on every wire you add, on every board: red = battery + / 6 V · orange = 5 V · black = GND · white = channel 1: motor M1 (AO1, BO1) and its direction input IN1 (AIN1 D7, BIN1 D4) · brown = channel 2: motor M2 (AO2, BO2) and IN2 (AIN2 D8, BIN2 D12) · purple = speed PWM (PWMA D6, PWMB D5) · green = encoder C1 (D2, D3) · yellow = encoder C2 (A0, A1) · grey = battery sense (VB → A6) · blue = sensors and serial (I2C, HC-SR04, HC-05, programming). White, green and yellow match the motor cable. Brown takes M2, because red is kept for the battery.

The six TB6612 control wires are now told apart: each motor channel is purple PWM · white IN1 · brown IN2, so IN1 shares white with the M1 output it drives and IN2 shares brown with M2. Left (A) and right (B) use the same three colors and never mix: on board 2 the A wires land in C11–C13 and the B wires in C15–C17, with the orange 5V between them. On JC the A wires are in row B and the B wires in row A. On board 3 each goes to its own Pro Mini pin.

Exception: the motors' own 6-wire cables keep the maker's colors: white M1 · blue GND · green C1 · yellow C2 · black VCC (5 V) · red M2. On this cable only, blue is GND, black is 5 V and red is M2. They end on board 0; from there on every wire follows the code above. The circuit diagram, the wiring table and all drawings now use these colors.

Eleven new drawings: board 0 top (with both cable ends) and underside (phase 03); board 2 connectors and cables, top and underside (phase 05); board 3 connectors, sockets and cables, top and four underside views (phase 06). MALE pins are drawn as gold squares on black, FEMALE contacts as dark holes (Dupont housing orange, socket black, JST white), every pin numbered with its signal; each cable is drawn from the holes it is soldered into to its female end, with the hole named under every housing pin. Every wire runs hole to hole in the circuit-diagram colors. Board 1 has nothing soldered to the board itself; its holder wiring is already drawn. Nothing below is final until you approve; the same changes then go to the v2 layout page.

  1. Checked against the circuit diagram: every wire's two ends against the connection table: power and star ground, all 5 V branches, battery sense, the six JC control lines (D4–D8, D12), motor outputs to J0-1…4, encoders (D2, D3, A0, A1), I2C (A4/A5), HC-SR04 (D9/D10), HC-05 and its divider. All match. An automatic check also confirms that no board 3 wire passes within 0.9 mm of a joint it doesn't belong to.
  2. One wire per hole. Where two wires used to share a hole, one moved. MP1584 #2's 5 V now lands in H10, and the switched BAT+ runs as a bus down column 20 (top lug → I20, bridged up to C20, which is the entry 100 µF +). Each wire on top leaves from its own bus hole.
  3. Changes on board 2: cable C now lands each wire straight over its TB6612 pin (C10 VB, C11–C17 control and 5V), so the underside needs only straight C–D–E bridges. Both cable C ground wires go into the star block (B18, B19). The VM 0.1 µF moved beside the 100 µF (M10–N10); only the VCC 0.1 µF stays under the module (J12–J13).
  4. Changes on board 3: the GY-521 SCL/SDA are reached through bridges K6–L6 and K7–L7. JC-10 and JE ground share the bridge B6–B7–B8. The HC-SR04 ground comes in through L12–M12–N12. The HC-05 divider node is the bridge L4–M4–N4. There are no splices anywhere: where two wires meet, they meet on a joint.
  5. Underside views are drawn as if you could see through the board from above, so the column numbers match the top view. When you turn the board over, left and right swap.
Always, every session. Never connect the USB-serial adapter's 5 V/VCC wire (only GND, TX, RX, DTR). Power the robot from the battery, also for uploads. Unplug the HC-05 before every upload. Switch off before plugging or unplugging any cable. Before every upload, check the robot's position (wheels in the air, or lying on its front). Never charge the cells inside the robot. Switch off right after use.

No BMS: these rules replace it. Keep the T3A fuse. Charge only outside the robot. Switch off after every session and never leave it on unattended. Check each cell now and then; stop using one that reads below 2.5 V or gets warm.

Changing the cells

  1. Switch off.
  2. Cell 1: slide it out to the front.
  3. Cell 2: untie the cable bundle at the back and swing it aside (or unplug cable B), then slide the cell out to the back. Tie the bundle again afterwards.

Taking the stack apart (top to bottom)

  1. Switch off, take both cells out.
  2. Board 3: unplug the HC-SR04, the HC-05, JC and JE; undo the 4 nylon nuts on top.
  3. Board 2: unplug JB and J0 at the back edge; undo the 2 mid-edge nuts. The fuse and cable C stay with board 2.
  4. Board 1: undo the 4 nylon nuts under board 0. Cables M and E stay on board 0.

No step needs desoldering. Full drawings and tables: v2 layout page.
