Balance Bot Rebuild Layout

Balance Bot Rebuild Layout

Four 4 × 6 cm boards, one function each, connected as a chain: every board has a cable that plugs into a socket on the board above. Parts: N20 6 V 300 rpm motors with Hall encoders, a TB6612FNG driver, a GY-521 MPU6050, your Arduino Pro Mini, an HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor, two MP1584 step-down regulators (6.0 V for the motors, 5.0 V for the logic), a 2 × 18650 holder in series (7.4 V, no boost regulators) and a reserved slot for an HC-05 Bluetooth module. Hole grid 2.54 mm, 20 columns × 14 rows (A–N), as in the article. Module sizes are typical values; measure the real parts when they arrive.

Overview

The stack, seen from the side

  • Chain: board 0 → cable M → socket J0 on board 1 → cable P → socket J1 on board 2 → cable L → socket J2 on board 3. Unplug any socket to take the boards apart.
  • Where the sockets are: J0 on top of board 1 at the front edge (cable M comes up around the front edge); J1 on top of board 2, running front to back in column 11 (cable P comes up around the back edge); J2 under board 3, back half (cable L goes straight up). Seen from the right side, front on the left.
  • Spacers: 25 mm between each pair of boards, so 12 spacers instead of the article's 8 (4 more needed). Height grows by about 25 mm, which raises the center of mass; that makes the robot fall more slowly. I will check it in the emulator with the measured mass and center of mass after the build.
  • Power and motor parts sit on board 1, directly above the motors (short motor wires). The logic on board 2 is one board further away from the motor noise.
Board 0 · motor board

Motors with encoders

  • No socket on this board. The two motors' wires (M1, M2) and encoder wires (VCC, GND, A, B) are joined into one 10-wire cable M with a plug that goes up to J0 on the power board.
  • Brackets stay in the same holes. The new motors have the same 12 × 10 mm housing, so the wheels stay where they are.
  • Each encoder adds about 8–10 mm at the back, so the free gap between the motors shrinks from about 28 mm to about 11 mm. This only works with the encoder plug on top; a plug pointing backward needs 5–10 mm more per motor.
  • The underside of this board stays empty.
Board 1 · power board

Motor driver and both regulators

  • TB6612 on the right, MP1584 #1 (6.0 V, motors) upper left, MP1584 #2 (5.0 V, logic) lower left, and the reused 10 µF capacitor across TB6612 VM/GND.
  • Socket J0 (10 pins, front edge) takes cable M from the motors. Cable P (14 wires, back edge) goes up to J1 on the logic board.
  • Star ground on this board: battery − arrives here (through J1 and cable P), and MP1584 #1, MP1584 #2 and the TB6612 each get their own ground wire to that point.
  • Set both MP1584 with a multimeter before plugging anything in: #1 to 6.0 V, #2 to 5.0 V. The trim screws stay reachable from the side of the stack.
  • The old MX1508 is removed.
Board 2 · logic board

Pro Mini and MPU6050

  • Arduino Pro Mini (5 V / 16 MHz) on the right, as in the article, pins in columns 13 and 19. Its 6-pin programming header points to the top edge, so the USB-serial adapter can still plug in for uploads and tests. A4/A5 (I2C) are inner pads.
  • The Pro Mini gets 5.0 V on VCC from MP1584 #2 on the power board (through J1). Its RAW pin and small onboard regulator stay unused.
  • Socket J1 (14 pins, column 11) takes cable P from the power board. Cable L (8 wires) goes up to J2 on the top board.
  • MPU6050 in the upper left, header in row F. The pins used are highlighted. The lower left stays free.
  • The MPU6050 is turned 90° compared with the article, so its X axis now points forward. The new program reads gyro Y and atan2(accX, accZ) instead of gyro X and atan2(accY, accZ). I will handle that in software and we will check the sign with the robot tilted forward before any test.
  • The MPU6050's INT pin stays unconnected. In the article it went to D2, which now belongs to the left encoder.
Board 3 · top board, underside

Sensors and the battery feed

  • The double holder covers the whole top side, so the ultrasonic sensor sits on the underside of the top board, at the front edge, still facing forward.
  • Socket J2 (8 pins, column 13) takes cable L from the logic board. It carries the switched battery (BAT+/BAT−) down the chain to the power board, and 5 V plus the sensor signals up.
  • No boost regulators. Both MP1584 step-down regulators are on the power board. The MT3608 boards are not used.
  • Reserved slot for an HC-05 Bluetooth module (ZS-040 board, about 37 × 16 mm), not fitted yet. Four wires from the Pro Mini (5V, GND, D0, D1) end in a 4-pin socket, so the module simply plugs in later. While nothing is plugged in, uploads and the serial monitor through the USB-serial adapter work as usual (the adapter uses the same D0/D1).
  • When it is enabled later: HC-05 TXD → D0 directly; Pro Mini D1 → 1 kΩ → HC-05 RXD → 2 kΩ → GND (the module's input is 3.3 V). Unplug the module for uploads.
Board 3 · top board, top side

2 × 18650 battery holder

  • The holder (about 77 × 41 × 20 mm) covers the full 40 mm board width and overhangs both ends, like the single holder did.
  • The cells are in series: the box has one red and one black lead, and the cells sit end to end. Check with a multimeter after inserting the cells: 7.4–8.4 V between the leads. If it reads 3.7–4.2 V, the box is parallel; tell me, and the circuit changes back.
  • The switch sits in the red lead, glued to the side of the holder or a spacer.
  • Charge both cells fully before inserting them, and use two cells of the same type and age. Stop using the robot below about 6.4 V (3.2 V per cell).
  • About 47 g more weight at the top raises the center of mass. That makes the robot fall more slowly, which helps; I will update the emulator with the new mass and height.
Circuit

Wiring diagram

  • Logic 5 V comes from MP1584 #2 (set to 5.0 V) into the Pro Mini's VCC pin. Load is about 60–100 mA (about 140 mA with the HC-05): Pro Mini, MPU6050, two encoders, TB6612 logic and the ultrasonic sensor. The Pro Mini's RAW pin and its small onboard regulator stay unused.
  • The two regulators at a glance (orange frames in the drawings): both on the power board. MP1584 #1, upper left, set to 6.0 V, feeds TB6612 VM (motors). MP1584 #2, lower left, set to 5.0 V, feeds Pro Mini VCC and the 5 V logic rail. The large black square on each board is its coil (marked 4R7 = 4.7 µH).
  • Motors get a constant 6.0 V from the MP1584 step-down regulator, their rated voltage, no matter how charged the battery is. No PWM cap is needed. Below about 7 V battery the output sags slightly (about 5.8 V at 6.5 V).
  • Never connect the USB-serial adapter's 5 V/VCC wire. Use only GND, TX, RX and DTR, and always power the robot from the battery (switch on), also for uploads. USB cannot supply the motors (about 0.5 A available, up to about 1.5 A needed). With the adapter's 5 V connected and the switch off, current would flow backward through MP1584 #2 into MP1584 #1 and the motors, overloading the adapter or the PC's USB port, and two 5 V supplies would fight when the switch is on.
  • Keep motor noise away from the logic (the article's lesson): reuse the 10 µF capacitor from the old bottom board across TB6612 VM / GND, right at the module, observing polarity. On the power board, give each MP1584 input its own wire from BAT+, and give MP1584 #1, MP1584 #2 and the TB6612 each their own ground wire to the point where BAT− arrives (star ground). Keep motor wires short, twisted in pairs, and away from SDA/SCL.
Connectors

Board-to-board chain

Each board has a cable soldered to it that plugs into a socket on the board above. Use 2.54 mm pin headers with Dupont housings; pin 1 is the square pin, mark it on the housing too. Power pins sit at one end of each connector, away from the encoder and sensor signals.

  • The switch and both battery leads stay on the top board. With any socket unplugged, every board below it is without power.
  • The encoder signals pass straight through the power board from J0 to cable P; only the motor wires and the encoder supply connect to parts there.
  • The 1k/2k divider for the HC-05 sits on the top board next to its socket, so cable L carries the plain D1 signal.
  • Dupont plugs can be reversed by mistake. Mark pin 1 on every housing and check with a multimeter before the first power-up: J1 and J2 pin 1 to pin 2 must not be a short.

  • Note 1: Toshiba asks for VCC before VM at power-up. Both regulators start from the same switch within milliseconds of each other, and the inputs have internal pull-downs, so the outputs stay off at power-up. If the motors ever twitch at switch-on, move STBY from 5 V to the free pin D11 and enable it in setup().
  • Note 2: No dropout figure is published. Expect the 6.0 V output to sag slightly when the battery falls below about 7 V; the 5.0 V output keeps margin down to the 6.4 V stop.
  • Note 3: The old library call blocks; the new program will trigger it from the timer, at most every 60 ms.
  • Note 4: Before connecting, check each motor's pins with a multimeter (continuity M1/M2 across the motor). If the encoder outputs have no pull-ups, the program enables the Pro Mini's internal ones.
